What follows if distance

Start with a simple premise: distinct positions are separated by at least a distance \(k\). Follow it through triangles, motion, lattice waves, and astronomical timing. Then ask how much of the prediction comes from the geometry—and how much comes from the dynamics we chose.

A small premise.
A chain of consequences.

Uniform discreteness is a precise mathematical assumption. A lattice, an interaction law, and an interpretation as photon propagation are additional choices. Each step changes what can be concluded.

1

Geometry

A Euclidean right triangle needs a hypotenuse at least \(\sqrt{2}k\). Bisection must stop. A continuous point trajectory from a connected time interval into this discrete space is constant.

2

A model of motion

Place continuous-time wave amplitudes on \(k\mathbb Z\). With symmetric positive couplings, short waves travel more slowly. The leading group-speed correction is quadratic in wave number.

3

A conditional test

If photons follow that wave law, high-energy photons acquire a propagation delay. Published astronomical timing limits constrain its coefficient. They do not establish a smallest distance.

From interactions
to a timing prediction

For finite-range positive couplings \(g_r\), the effective interaction length \(\Lambda\) is at least the lattice spacing. Nearest neighbors give \(\Lambda=k\). The quadratic expansion applies at sufficiently low wave number.

$$\ddot u_n=\sum_{r=1}^{R}g_r(u_{n+r}-2u_n+u_{n-r})$$
$$\Lambda^2=k^2\frac{\sum_r g_r r^4}{\sum_r g_r r^2}\geq k^2$$
$$\frac{v_g}{c}=1-\frac{\Lambda^2q^2}{8}+O(q^4)$$
The scale gap

About 60 million
Planck lengths

The strongest translated limit is still about \(6.1\times10^7\ell_P\). For Planck spacing and nearest neighbors, the illustrative GRB delay is only \(3.2\times10^{-15}\) seconds.

That is roughly \(3.8\times10^{15}\) below the delay coefficient at the published limit.

observations say?

Keep the spacing.
Change the dynamics.

The follow-up stays on the same lattice. Standard centered finite-difference couplings, including negative pair couplings, have a positive total energy and suppress dispersion to arbitrarily high order as the interaction range grows.

Positive interactions have a limit

Nearest neighbors maximize phase speed among positive symmetric finite-range rules at fixed long-wave speed.

Stability allows more

The centered signed family has nonnegative energy and group speed between zero and \(c\). Its error begins at order \((qk)^{2R}\).

The cutoff still matters

No finite-range rule exactly reproduces continuum dispersion on an open interval. Convergence of group speed is restricted to bands strictly below the zone edge.

This is a model calculation, not experimental evidence. The finite-difference formulas are established numerical mathematics. A group-speed cap and energy stability do not supply a complete relativistic theory.

Where this leaves the question

A minimum distance needs a physical model.

The observations leave Planck-spacing versions of the positive-coupling model far beyond reach. The dynamics study adds another lesson: minimum spacing alone does not enforce a detectable quadratic delay. Further tests need additional physical assumptions about interactions, matter, or cosmology before they can make specific predictions.
