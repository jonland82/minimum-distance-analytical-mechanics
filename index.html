What follows if distance
has a lower limit?

Jonathan R. Landers

Suppose distinct positions are separated by at least \(k\). That premise puts a threshold on which triangles fit at a given size and gives repeated routing queries a way to skip a scan. It also motivates lattice-wave models whose predictions can be compared with photon timing.

One premise, several questions

The cutoff constrains distances directly. Counting shapes requires a sampling law, routing adds an optimization objective, and physical predictions require a model of how waves propagate.

Which shapes survive?

A triangle survives when its shortest side reaches \(k\). Count exclusions under two shape distributions, then use the same threshold to index candidate routes.

How do waves travel?

Positive couplings enforce a leading quadratic slowdown in the lattice model. Stable signed couplings can suppress it to arbitrarily high order.

From triangles to routing

Fix a triangle?s shape. If \(S\) is the ratio of its shortest to longest side, the minimum surviving diameter is \(k/S\). Vary the shape to count exclusions, or vary the cutoff to query a fixed list of routes.

How large must a triangle be to survive?

At every diameter \(D>k\), the cutoff excludes a greater fraction of angle-uniform right triangles than of triangles sampled uniformly by area in normalized side-length coordinates. Their mean required diameters are infinite and \(4k\log 2\), respectively.

From interactions
to a timing prediction

For finite-range positive couplings \(g_r\), the effective interaction length \(\Lambda\) is at least the lattice spacing. Nearest neighbors give \(\Lambda=k\). The quadratic expansion applies at sufficiently low wave number.

$$\ddot u_n=\sum_{r=1}^{R}g_r(u_{n+r}-2u_n+u_{n-r})$$
$$\Lambda^2=k^2\frac{\sum_r g_r r^4}{\sum_r g_r r^2}\geq k^2$$
$$\frac{v_g}{c}=1-\frac{\Lambda^2q^2}{8}+O(q^4)$$
The scale gap

Planck lengths

The strongest translated limit is still about \(6.1\times10^7\ell_P\). For Planck spacing and nearest neighbors, the illustrative GRB delay is only \(3.2\times10^{-15}\) seconds.

That is roughly \(3.8\times10^{15}\) below the delay coefficient at the published limit.

Group speed on a fixed lattice

Choose an interaction range to compare the group speeds.

Positive interactions have a limit

Nearest neighbors maximize phase speed among positive symmetric finite-range rules at fixed long-wave speed.

Stability allows more

The centered signed family has nonnegative energy and group speed between zero and \(c\). Its error begins at order \((qk)^{2R}\).

The cutoff still matters

No finite-range rule exactly reproduces continuum dispersion on an open interval. Convergence of group speed is restricted to bands strictly below the zone edge.

This is a model calculation, not experimental evidence. The finite-difference formulas are established numerical mathematics. A group-speed cap and energy stability do not supply a complete relativistic theory.
