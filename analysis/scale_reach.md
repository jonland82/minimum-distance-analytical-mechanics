# Can experiments reach Planck spacing in this model?

Literature assessment: November 13, 2026.

For the paper's nearest-neighbor photon model with k = Lambda = lP, no demonstrated experiment or established forecast located in this search reaches the predicted quadratic timing effect. This is a statement about the particular signal, not a claim that every conceivable Planck-scale effect is inaccessible.

## Numerical size

Using the previously calculated GRB 221009A kernel K2 = 7.7077839e16 s and EPlanck = 1.2208901e19 GeV, the nearest-neighbor Planck prediction is delta_t = K2 (E/EPlanck)^2 / 8, relative to a negligible low energy. Representative observed photon energies give:

| Energy | Predicted delay |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 TeV | 6.46e-17 s |
| 1 PeV | 6.46e-11 s |

For that same hypothetical distance/redshift kernel, a 1 ms delay would require about 3.93e18 eV photons; a 1 s delay would require about 1.24e20 eV photons. These are scale estimates, not forecasts of observable sources or usable photon statistics. Timing-source emission uncertainty is part of the sensitivity problem, beyond detector clock resolution.

The conventional quadratic scale corresponding to Lambda = lP is sqrt(12) EPlanck = 4.23e19 GeV. The LHAASO collaboration timing result used earlier reaches 6.9e11 GeV. The gap in effective length is about 6.2e7, or 3.8e15 in the delay coefficient. Repeating weaker source comparisons cannot by itself bridge that difference.

The distinction between k and Lambda matters: Planck spacing with sufficiently long positive interactions could produce a larger, accessible Lambda. That would test those interaction choices; it would not directly resolve Planck-spaced positions. The low-energy expansion must still apply across the observed band.

## Other Planck-sensitive observables

- **Optical polarization:** Kislat and Krawczynski, PRD 95, 083013 (2017), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.95.083013), constrain dimension-five, birefringent photon-sector effects, reporting an effective energy-scale limit above the Planck energy. The polarization rotation is quadratic in energy, while the associated velocity effect is linear. Neither is the paper's polarization-independent quadratic group-speed correction. No polarization prediction is supplied by the scalar lattice model.
- **Holometer:** Chou et al., CQG 34, 165005 (2017), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6382/aa7bd3), test specific correlated geometrical shear-noise models at Planck-normalized noise levels. This does not constitute spatial imaging at 1e-35 m. A fixed position gap does not specify a noise spectrum or the needed cross-correlations.
- **Quantum optomechanics:** Pikovski et al., Nature Physics 8, 393–397 (2012), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys2262), propose a quantum-optical test of modified mechanical commutators. It is a proposed model-dependent route, not a demonstrated measurement of this distance gap. The paper does not derive such commutator modifications.
- **High-energy photon interactions:** Satunin, EPJC 81, 750 (2021), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-021-09547-1), uses air-shower suppression to constrain subluminal dispersion more strongly than the timing scale used here, but still below the required Planck energy scale. Applying it requires a photon-electron interaction theory. It is a more relevant next theoretical extension than borrowing polarization or geometrical-noise bounds without deriving their signals.

The practical next step is to specify an interacting photon/matter theory and derive its observable effects before identifying applicable stronger limits. An experiment's sensitivity to one Planck-normalized parameter cannot be transferred to a different observable or mathematical realization of minimum length.
