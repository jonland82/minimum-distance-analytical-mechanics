# Other published tests relevant to the minimum-distance model

Literature checked November 13, 2026. This is a literature assessment; none of these additional datasets has been downloaded or independently fitted here.

## Closest matches: quadratic, subluminal photon timing

These tests constrain the additional photon propagation model, rather than the minimum-distance geometric hypothesis by itself. Bounds inherit the observational source and detector assumptions. For the standard quadratic convention used in our translation, EQG2 = sqrt(12) hbar c / Lambda. Each paper's convention must be checked before numerical reuse.

Less sensitive than GRB 221009A, but useful for testing whether a timing pattern persists across source classes. Intrinsic energy-dependent pulse phase remains a nuisance. |

Useful for a common propagation coefficient across distinct source distances, with independent source-lag models. |
| MAGIC Collaboration, JCAP 07, 044 (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2024/07/044), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07140) | Mrk 421 flare of 2014; 95% subluminal quadratic lower limit EQG > 2.6e10 GeV | Another blazar observation and an alternative analysis approach, useful as a cross-check. |

The three timing limits above are weaker in energy-scale reach than the LHAASO GRB result already translated. Their value is independent replication and variation of source physics, rather than improved Planck-scale sensitivity. Publication access does not establish public availability of all event-level data and detector-response files; that must be checked separately.

## A published lag that is not established propagation evidence

MAGIC Collaboration and collaborators, Physics Letters B 668, 253–257 (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physletb.2008.08.053), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2889), analyzed a Mrk 501 flare. They reported a quadratic lag coefficient of (3.71 +/- 2.57)e-6 s/GeV^2. The better-known 2.5-sigma result refers to a linear-energy fit, not the quadratic effect predicted here. The quadratic estimate is only about 1.4 statistical standard deviations from zero. The paper explicitly leaves intrinsic source explanations open. This is a useful source-lag case study, not affirmative evidence for this theory.

## Different observables requiring a more complete model

**High-energy air showers and photon spectra.** Satunin, EPJC 81, 750 (2021), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-021-09547-1), derives a subluminal scale bound M_LV > 1.7e13 GeV using Tibet-AS gamma observations and shower-formation suppression in an effective field theory. This is a potentially strong complementary route. It assumes photon-electron interaction dynamics that are absent from the scalar lattice model. It cannot be imported directly as a constraint on k or Lambda without deriving the corresponding interaction theory. Photon-decay limits on the opposite, superluminal sign likewise do not automatically constrain the present subluminal model.

**Laboratory direction-dependence tests.** Nagel et al., Nature Communications 6, 8174 (2015), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9174), used precision oscillators in a modern Michelson–Morley experiment. They found no significant direction-dependent signal, reporting relative frequency variation (9.2 +/- 10.7)e-19 at 95% confidence. A three-dimensional lattice may predict orientation dependence, but the paper's one-dimensional model supplies no definite angular law or resonator/matter response. Those predictions would have to be developed before using this experiment quantitatively. The quoted frequency precision is not a direct spatial-resolution measurement.

## Suggested next step

Use the Crab pulsar and PKS 2155-304 as independent timing comparisons. Translate published likelihoods or confidence intervals into Lambda only after checking conventions. Before a new event-level fit, confirm access to event data, backgrounds, and instrument responses. A joint analysis should fit a common propagation coefficient with source-dependent emission-lag nuisance parameters; it should not treat observed lags as pure propagation.

For a genuinely different and potentially stronger test, first extend the photon model to specify interactions with electrons, then assess the Tibet-AS/LHAASO shower constraints. None of these measurements directly establishes continuous geometry or sub-Planck spatial resolution.
